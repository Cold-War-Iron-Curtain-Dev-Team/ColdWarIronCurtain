# Faction Rework: Next Phase for Approval

## Recommendation

**Represent military commitments, not every international organization, as factions.** Prioritize four content packages: **SEATO, Baghdad Pact/CENTO, the Rio Pact, and Gulf military cooperation**. Give each its own identity and objectives, but create a separate engine faction only where membership can be represented without breaking an existing alliance.

The central constraint is overlapping membership. The vanilla documentation states that `can_leader_join_other_factions` **merges/destroys the old faction**; it does not permit dual membership. The USA cannot historically lead NATO, SEATO, ANZUS, and the Rio Pact as four independent factions.

Accordingly:

- Preserve NATO and its existing GUI; preserve Comecon as a separate economic system.
- Represent overlapping treaties through **existing membership ideas, decisions, and events**, with treaty-specific objectives.
- Reserve standalone templates for compatible regional commands and explicit alternate-history alliances.
- Do not manufacture faction leaders or remove countries from their established alliances merely to populate a new template.

All names, thresholds, and rewards below are **proposed design**, not claims about existing implementation.

### Rio membership must be date-sensitive

The **1949 bookmark's deposited ratifications** cover Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Haiti, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, USA, and Uruguay.

Argentina, Bolivia, Ecuador, and Peru ratified in 1950; Guatemala in 1955; Trinidad and Tobago joined in 1967; the Bahamas in 1982. Venezuela was an original participant, but its present-day depositary entry reflects later denunciation and re-accession: do not use that current entry alone to reconstruct historical membership.

Do not confuse **OAS membership, treaty signature, ratification, participation in OAS organs, and operational military cooperation**.

## 2. Four Priority Content Packages

### Shared goal conventions

- **S/M/L** mean short-, medium-, and long-term.
- A genuine faction goal awards **1/2/3 faction initiative**, respectively, **plus the listed reward**.
- Treaty-only versions award the listed reward, **not initiative to an unrelated NATO or other faction**.
- PP rewards go to participating governments; service XP goes only to countries that supplied the relevant exercise or operational contribution.
- Each objective is one-shot. Contribution goals require an actual commitment, delivery, or completed activity, not clicking an acceptance option.
- Historical withdrawal and dissolution cancel inapplicable goals. An alternate-history continuation requires an explicit political decision.
- Manifest concepts below are continuous measures of cooperation, not annexation targets or additional national spirits by default.

### A. SEATO

**Manifest: “Consultation Before Commitment”**

Measure functioning cooperation among treaty participants: active liaison arrangements, regional exercises, and agreed assistance. Do not measure the percentage of Asia that has become non-communist.

| Term | Goal | Completion in plain terms | Additional reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | **Establish the Bangkok Liaison Network** | Thailand and at least three other participants complete and fund their liaison commitments. | +25 PP |
| S | **Hold a Combined Exercise** | Thailand or the Philippines hosts an exercise with actual contributions from at least two other participants. | +10 relevant service XP |
| M | **Deliver the Regional Assistance Programme** | Complete agreed equipment or training deliveries to both Thailand and the Philippines. | +15 army XP to contributors |
| M | **Agree an Indochina Response** | A qualifying Indochina crisis receives a recorded consultation outcome, and participating governments deliver the assistance they pledged. Beneficiary states need not become members. | +35 PP |
| L | **Sustain Regional Cooperation** | For three years after the preceding cooperation goals, both Southeast Asian members retain active participation and at least one further funded programme is completed. | +50 PP |

**Rules:** accession by invitation and agreement, with explicit alternate-history routes for additional members; no automatic admission of protocol beneficiaries. US sponsorship is not a historical right to command every member's forces. Withdrawals remain possible. Internal insurgency prompts consultation; **Pakistan's wars with India do not automatically become SEATO wars**. Historical dissolution in 1977 unless a continuation path has been chosen.

### B. Baghdad Pact / CENTO

**Manifest: “A Northern Tier of Security”**

Measure maintained defence consultation, communications, and agreed cooperation among current participants. Do not require the conquest of neighbouring neutral states.

| Term | Goal | Completion in plain terms | Additional reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | **Convene the Pact Council** | Iraq and Turkey establish the organization and at least two further historical participants formally accede. | +25 PP |
| S | **Connect the Regional Staffs** | Turkey, Iran, and Pakistan complete funded military liaison and communications commitments. | +10 army XP |
| M | **Exercise the Northern Tier** | At least three regional participants complete a combined exercise and provide the pledged forces or expenditure. | +15 relevant service XP |
| M | **Carry the Secretariat to Ankara** | Following an Iraqi withdrawal, the remaining governments expressly renew cooperation and establish the successor organization. Only available on that branch. | +35 PP |
| L | **Keep the Regional Commitments** | Turkey, Iran, and Pakistan maintain active cooperation for three years and complete a further joint programme during that period. | +50 PP |

**Rules:** treaty accession, not adjacency or a generic ideology checkbox. Iraq is the founding host, **not an irrevocable leader of CENTO after withdrawing**. A standalone regional variant requires an agreed coordinating government; no manpower takeover. Britain and the USA can sponsor cooperation without leaving NATO; the USA is not silently promoted to full historical membership. No automatic collective intervention in Indo-Pakistani wars. Historical collapse in 1979 unless an explicit survival branch applies.

### C. Rio Pact / TIAR

**Manifest: “Consultation in Defence of the Americas”**

Measure participating governments' ability to consult and implement agreed measures against a defined threat. Internal disputes should reduce cohesion rather than generate conquest rewards.

| Term | Goal | Completion in plain terms | Additional reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | **Establish the Consultation Procedure** | At least two-thirds of current ratifying participants complete the initial consultation arrangements. | +25 PP |
| S | **Prepare Maritime Liaison** | Three participating coastal states complete funded naval liaison or a joint maritime exercise. | +10 naval XP |
| M | **Submit the Dispute to Mediation** | A qualifying inter-American dispute ends through an accepted settlement without territorial annexation by the mediator. | +35 PP |
| M | **Implement the Agreed Measures** | A qualifying crisis receives the required collective decision and at least three governments carry out their pledged measures. Military action additionally requires each contributor's consent. | +35 PP |
| L | **Maintain the Inter-American Peace** | After a mediated crisis, participating states remain free of war against one another for three years and complete a further consultation or exercise programme. | +50 PP |

**Rules:** eligibility follows treaty accession and ratification, not automatic inclusion of every American country. Political consultation uses treaty procedures; a US administrative role is not unrestricted sovereign command. **Article 20 expressly protects a state's consent to using armed force.** No automatic hemispheric war over every US intervention, and no automatic common position in a dispute involving two American states. The Falklands crisis must permit divided responses rather than a scripted USA-UK war.

### D. GCC / Peninsula Shield

**Manifest: “Defence Cooperation in the Gulf”**

Measure the six governments' practical participation in liaison, exercises, and a jointly supported force. Economic integration is not the manifest.

| Term | Goal | Completion in plain terms | Additional reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | **Convene the Defence Representatives** | All six GCC governments approve liaison arrangements after the organization's actual foundation. | +25 PP |
| S | **Agree the Common Exercise Plan** | At least four governments fund and complete their preparations for the first combined exercise. | +10 army XP |
| M | **Exercise Peninsula Shield** | A Saudi-hosted exercise is completed with actual contingents from at least three other members. | +15 army XP |
| M | **Establish the Joint Force** | After the appropriate early-1980s political decisions, the host provides facilities and participating governments deliver their pledged contingents and funding. | +15 army XP |
| L | **Maintain a Deployable Shield** | The joint force remains funded for two years, conducts a subsequent exercise, and retains participation from at least four governments. | +50 PP |

**Rules:** restrict historical membership to the six GCC states; external powers are partners, not members. Saudi Arabia is the proposed **engine coordinating leader** because it hosts the force, not because the GCC constitution establishes Saudi supremacy. Leadership changes require a collective political event, not army size. Deployments require an agreed mission and participating governments' consent. The Iran-Iraq War does not itself compel GCC entry; a 1990 Kuwait crisis must respect actual occupation and participation choices.

## 3. Generic Template Policy

The existing generic manifest, **Strength in Unity**, measures membership against a target of **20 countries**. That is unsuitable for a two-country emergency coalition and encourages irrelevant expansion.

### Proposed baseline

| Component | Policy |
|---|---|
| **Manifest** | **“The Coalition's Common Undertaking.”** Track the continued participation and survival of the original signatories; use a fixed founding roster so expansion or expulsion cannot inflate progress. Keep any cohesion benefit modest. |
| **Default short-term goal** | **“Establish Joint Liaison”**: at least two founding participants complete a funded coordination commitment. Reward: **1 initiative +10 army XP** for contributors. No immediate reward for merely creating the faction. |
| **Campaign medium-term goal** | Creation script selects **one appropriate objective**: repel a specified invasion, preserve a threatened government, secure an agreed withdrawal, or defeat a named opponent. Reward: **2 initiative +25 PP**. Never infer the objective from a faction's name. |
| **Long-term goal** | **None by default.** A durable coalition may receive **“Conclude the Settlement”** only when its owner content defines an observable settlement. Reward: **3 initiative +50 PP**. |
| **Expansion and ideology** | No compulsory recruitment quota, worldwide ideological goal, nuclear race, or generic territorial-conquest objective. |
| **Ending the coalition** | Use an explicit dissolution or continuation decision once its undertaking ends. Dissolve only the coalition that the responsible content actually created. |

A generic coalition is not inherently defensive. Congo interventions, insurgent fronts, and emergency interstate alliances require different purposes; the owner must select the appropriate war policy.

### Legacy call sites

**Migrate the approximately twelve legacy `create_faction` call sites**, since vanilla documents that effect as obsolete. Migration should preserve each coalition's historical name, participants, intended duration, and political purpose.

Audit the approximately sixty generic-template callers at the same time:

1. **Durable alliance:** assign a suitable dedicated template.
2. **Specific campaign coalition:** retain generic, but supply its defined objective and rules.
3. **Diplomatic organization or assistance relationship:** do not create a faction solely for display.
4. **Existing-faction participant:** preserve its alliance and represent participation separately.

Never dismantle NATO, the Warsaw Pact, or another established faction to make a temporary coalition work. Apply the same protection to prospective members, not just the intended coalition leader.

## 4. Cross-Bloc Rules Policy

| Area | Proposed policy |
|---|---|
| **Defensive commitments** | Gate calls by **the specific qualifying war**, protected participant, geographic scope, and authorization. “The caller is fighting some defensive war” is insufficient when it also has an unrelated offensive war. |
| **Consultative treaties** | Consultation is not automatic war entry. SEATO, CENTO, ANZUS, FPDA, and Rio participation must preserve the distinctions between political agreement, assistance, and armed intervention. |
| **National war declarations** | Do not universally prohibit members from declaring their own wars. Prevent unauthorized wars from acquiring alliance-wide obligations. Separate sanctions or expulsion may follow through political content. |
| **Leadership** | Lock NATO to the USA. Recommend a distinct Soviet leadership lock for the Warsaw Pact. Preserve designated theatre commanders during their mandates. Do not use manpower or industrial thresholds to transfer treaty institutions to an ahistorical hegemon. |
| **Joining** | Use treaty-specific eligibility and political acceptance. Geography, independence, rival obligations, and established country paths matter more than broad ideology labels. |
| **Ideology** | No universal democratic-only Western rule: historical alliances included authoritarian governments. Communist-only eligibility may suit particular Soviet blocs, but must not collapse Sino-Soviet relations into automatic subordination. NAM is not an ideology faction. |
| **Dismissal** | No automatic expulsion merely because a member begins an offensive war; otherwise a sovereign action can inadvertently void its other commitments. Reserve expulsion, suspension, and withdrawal for explicit political conditions and decisions. |
| **Subjects and beneficiaries** | Do not automatically admit colonial dependencies, protectorates, protocol beneficiaries, medical contributors, or aid recipients as sovereign treaty members. |
| **Scripted membership** | Apply the same political safeguards to scripted additions and war entry as to player diplomacy. Do not assume a selectable rule's availability prevents scripts from setting or bypassing it. |
| **Bookmark initialization** | **1949:** initialize only institutions and ratified commitments already active. **1980:** SEATO and CENTO are already dissolved; GCC and RSS do not yet exist. The Sino-Soviet treaty is near expiry but does not imply a functioning alliance. |
| **Rewards** | Small, one-shot cooperation rewards; no repeated annual initiative farming or stacked permanent combat bonuses for overlapping treaties. Never pay the same historical contribution through several packages. |

## 5. Implementation Notes for Approval

- Existing `SEATO_member` and `CENTO_member` ideas already support overlapping affiliations and are read by UN voting content. **Reuse and reconcile these**, rather than adding an independent membership convention.
- The existing Rio, Peninsula Shield, and Regional Security System template owner files are empty. Fill the appropriate owners only after approving representation; filenames alone do not establish a working faction.
- The RSS owner filename begins `CUB_`; historical RSS leadership must not be inferred from that filename.
- **Unfixed key problem:** `Establish_SEATO.2` adds `SEATO_Member`, while the defined idea is `SEATO_member`; no exact `SEATO_Member` definition was found.
- No repository edits or verification runs were performed.
